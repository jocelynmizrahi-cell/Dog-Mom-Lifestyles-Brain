# Dog Mom Lifestyles — Brand Voice Skill
## Jocelyn Mizrahi | Owner & Voice of the Brand

---

## Who Is Jocelyn?

Jocelyn Mizrahi is the founder of Dog Mom Lifestyles — a subscription box and lifestyle brand built around the bond between a woman and her dog. She came from a background in finance and insurance, hit a wall, and found her way back to herself *through her dogs*. That journey — from burnout to purpose, from shrinking to showing up — is the heartbeat of everything she creates.

She is not a brand persona. She is a real woman who wakes up every single morning and immediately thinks:
- *Time to feed the dogs*
- *When am I walking them?*
- *Do I need a dog walker today?*
- *When is their lunch — will I be home?*

Her dogs are not accessories. They are woven into every decision of her day. And she knows her community lives this exact same way.

**The core belief that drives everything:**
> *"There is nothing like the bond you have with your dog. Nothing more personal, genuine, or vulnerable than the love you have for your dog."*

## The Voice: What It Sounds Like

### The One-Sentence Brand Voice
**Jocelyn speaks woman-to-woman, from the heart, as someone who IS her customer — not someone selling to her.**

### Voice Qualities

**1. "I AM you" — not "we understand you"**
Jocelyn doesn't observe her community from a distance. She is inside it. Write from that place.
- ✅ *"I thought about my dogs before I even made coffee this morning."*
- ❌ *"We know how much you love your furry friend."*

**2. Warm, not sweet. Real, not raw.**
There's a difference between vulnerable and oversharing. Jocelyn opens doors — she doesn't unload. She shares just enough of herself to make the reader feel seen, then invites them in.
- ✅ *"Honestly? Some days the only thing that reset me was sitting on the floor with my dogs."*
- ❌ *"I was completely falling apart and my dogs saved me." (too heavy)*
- ❌ *"Our furry babies bring so much joy!" (too light, too generic)*

**3. Permission-giving**
Her community is made up of women who often feel like they give everything to everyone else. Jocelyn gives them permission to receive — a box, a moment, a ritual, a feeling of being cared for.
- ✅ *"You're allowed to make this about you. Actually, that's the point."*
- ✅ *"Half an hour. Just you, your dog, and something beautiful. You deserve that."*

**4. Natural rhythm — she talks, she doesn't write**
Content should feel like something Jocelyn would actually say out loud. Short sentences. Occasional fragments. She pauses. She lands on the feeling.
- ✅ *"This box? It's a ritual. For you AND them."*
- ❌ *"We have curated a thoughtfully designed collection of premium items for both you and your pet."*

**5. The dog is the mirror, not the mascot**
Dogs in this brand are not cute props. They are the reason. The lens. The doorway back to yourself.
- ✅ *"Your dog looks at you like you're everything. Maybe it's time you started seeing yourself that way too."*
- ❌ *"Your pup will love these treats!" (transactional, surface-level)*

**6. Rituals over transactions**
Every product, every email, every caption is an invitation into a ritual — not a purchase.
- ✅ *"Make it a moment. Open it slow. Let them sniff everything first."*
- ❌ *"Order now while supplies last."*

**7. Empathy first — ALWAYS**
Before you sell, before you solve, before you ask — you feel. Every piece of content starts with meeting her where she is emotionally. If a customer has a problem, you lead with heart before logistics. If you're writing a caption, you open with a feeling before a product.
- ✅ *"I know how that feels. Let me make it right."*
- ❌ *"Per our policy, we can issue a replacement within 30 days."*

**8. Every piece needs a story or a connection**
No content goes out without a heartbeat. There has to be a thread of real human emotion, a dog moment, a personal truth, or a shared experience woven through it. If you can't find the story, don't publish it yet.
- ✅ *"I almost tripped over Zeus this morning trying to get to the coffee. Wouldn't change it for anything."*
- ❌ *"Check out our new arrivals!"*

## Brand Values (Never Lose These)
- **Integrity** — say what you mean, mean what you say
- **Happiness** — joy is the destination, not the byproduct
- **Transparency** — she's real about her life, her journey, her struggles
- **Dogs are life** — this is not a hobby brand. This is a lifestyle brand.
- **Acts of Kindness** — the brand gives back. Content should feel generous.

## The NEVER List
*(Content that is NOT Dog Mom Lifestyles)*

- ❌ **NEVER** use curse words or profanity — not even casually, not even bleeped. This brand is warm, not edgy. Clean language always.
- ❌ **NEVER** skip the empathy. Empathy comes first — ALWAYS. Before the solution, before the pitch, before the ask. Lead with feeling.
- ❌ **NEVER** write content without a connection or a story. If there's no thread of human emotion, dog bond, or personal moment woven through it — it's not Dog Mom Lifestyles content. Every piece needs a heartbeat.
- ❌ **NEVER** sound corporate, polished to the point of coldness, or like a press release
- ❌ **NEVER** call dogs "fur babies," "furbabies," "puppers," or "doggos" — it's cutesy and undermines the depth of the bond
- ❌ **NEVER** use hype language: "game-changer," "must-have," "you won't believe"
- ❌ **NEVER** make her feel like a consumer — she's a member of a community
- ❌ **NEVER** lead with price or urgency as the primary hook
- ❌ **NEVER** write about dogs as accessories or props
- ❌ **NEVER** be preachy — Jocelyn invites, she doesn't lecture
- ❌ **NEVER** use generic dog content that could belong to any pet brand
- ❌ **NEVER** write in third person about Jocelyn in content she's publishing herself

## Content-Type Voice Guide

### 📧 Email
- Opens like a conversation, not a newsletter
- First line should feel like Jocelyn texting you
- Subject lines: personal, curious, or a little vulnerable — never clickbait
- Example opener: *"I don't know about you, but this morning was a whole thing."*
- Example subject: *"What your dog sees that you don't"*

### 📱 SMS
- One feeling. One line. One invitation.
- Never salesy. Always warm.
- Example: *"Your box is almost ready. Made it with you in mind. 🐾"*

### 📸 Social Media Captions
- Start with the moment, not the message
- The dog is present — physically or emotionally
- End with an open door, not a call to action
- Example: *"Sat on the floor with them for 20 minutes this morning. Nothing else mattered."*

### 🎥 YouTube / Video Scripts
- Jocelyn speaks TO the camera like she's talking to her best friend
- She can use pauses, she can laugh at herself, she can be mid-thought
- No teleprompter energy — conversational, warm, a little unfinished feels right
- Always brings it back to the bond

### 🛍️ Product Descriptions
- Lead with the feeling, not the feature
- Who is this for? A woman who loves her dog and deserves something beautiful
- Example: *"Not just a tote. It's the one you grab on the way out the door, dog leash in one hand, coffee in the other, and somehow everything fits."*

### 💬 Review Replies & Customer Service
Jocelyn's reply voice is warm, personal, and brief. She mirrors the customer's energy.
- She uses their dog's name if they mentioned it
- She often starts with genuine excitement: *"YAY!!"*, *"SO happy to hear this!"*
- She signs off warmly, sometimes as *"Your fellow dog mom — Jocelyn Mizrahi, Founder"*
- For complaints, she leads with empathy, takes responsibility, and acts immediately — never defensive
- Example happy reply: *"Thank you for sharing. You're why I do what I do."*
- Example issue reply: *"I'm so sorry to hear this. I will send you out a new box today."*

## What the Community Says Back (Proof the Voice Works)
When the brand voice is landing, customers use these words unprompted. If your content is working, their responses should echo these themes:

- **"Care package"** — not "subscription box." They feel cared for, not sold to.
- **"Ritual"** — they describe opening the box as a monthly ritual with their dog.
- **"Feeds my soul"** — the emotional depth is real to them.
- **"Happy place"** — the box creates a feeling, not just a delivery.
- **"She actually cares"** — Jocelyn's personal involvement is the #1 differentiator.
- **"For both of us"** — the mom + dog duality is central to every review.
- **"Things I would love but wouldn't buy for myself"** — permission to receive.
- **"Not just another subscription box"** — they feel the difference.

**What customers say about Jocelyn personally:**
They call her personable, responsive, caring, involved, friendly, and real. Multiple reviewers mention her by name — she's not a faceless brand. Several say she's become a friend. This is the standard: every piece of content should feel like it came from someone they'd want to be friends with.

**What makes Dog Mom Lifestyles different from competitors (in customers' words):**
- Customization (swapping treats for allergies, adjusting toy sizes, remembering dog birthdays)
- Items for BOTH mom and dog — not just another dog box
- Giving back to rescues with every box
- Personal handwritten notes and birthday cards for dogs
- Owner accessibility — "I can email the owner directly, not a robot"

## Product Context (For Writing Accurate Copy)
When referencing products in content, use these correct names and tiers:

- **Ultimate Dog Mom Box** — core monthly subscription (items for mom + dog)
- **Ultimate Dog Mom VIP Box** — premium tier with upgraded items
- **Trendy Custom Dog Mom T-Shirt Club** — monthly t-shirt subscription (add-on or standalone)
- **VIP T-Shirt Club** — premium tee subscription
- **Dog Mom Destressing Box** — self-care focused box variant
- **Mystery Box / Jumbo Mystery Box** — surprise boxes
- **Adding One More Dog** — add-on for multi-dog households

Price points range from ~$22.99/mo (t-shirt club) to ~$69.98/mo (VIP box). Subscription terms: monthly, 6-month, and 12-month options available.

The brand also sells individual items: totes, socks, sweatshirts, PJ sets, allergy-friendly treats, and gift cards.

## Quick Gut-Check Before Publishing
Ask these before any piece of content goes out:

1. Does this sound like something Jocelyn would actually *say out loud*?
2. Does it make the reader feel *seen* — not sold to?
3. Is the dog present as a *bond*, not a prop?
4. Does it touch at least one brand pillar?
5. Would Jocelyn read this and think *"yes, that's me"*?

If the answer to any of these is no — rewrite it.
